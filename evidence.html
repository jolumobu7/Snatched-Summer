Training

Expert consensusv1 · checked 2026-11-23

When you finish every set at the target, the next session adds a small amount of weight (or reps). Too hard? It backs off.

  • ACSM, 2009 · American College of Sports Medicine position stand. Progression models in resistance training for healthy adults. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 2009. position stand · Adults (general)
    Progressive overload is a core principle: training needs to get gradually harder to keep producing results, applied to each person’s goals and training status.

Limits: The exact step sizes (5 lb lower body, 2.5 lb upper body) are our practical choice, not numbers from the position stand.

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Women follow the same core strength principles as men: progressive lifting, full-body, 2+ days a week.

  • Roberts et al., 2020 · Sex Differences in Resistance Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 2020. meta-analysis · Women and men
    Women and men gained muscle and lower-body strength at similar relative rates on the same programs; women gained relatively more upper-body strength.

Limits: This analysis covers young to middle-aged adults. Results for older women can differ when changes are compared in absolute terms.

Strong evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Plans run 2–5 training days a week, with optional walking and easy cardio on other days.

  • Bull et al. (WHO), 2020 · World Health Organization 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2020. guideline · Adults (general)
    Adults should do muscle-strengthening activity for all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week, plus 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity.

Limits: These are general health guidelines for adults, not a plan for maximum muscle growth.

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Big lifts rest about 2–3 minutes; small moves rest 45–90 seconds. Short sessions cut exercises before they cut rest.

  • Schoenfeld et al., 2016 · Longer Interset Rest Periods Enhance Muscle Strength and Hypertrophy in Resistance-Trained Men. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 2016. randomized trial · Men only
    3-minute rests produced more strength and muscle growth than 1-minute rests in trained men.
  • Grgic et al., 2017 · The effects of short versus long inter-set rest intervals in resistance training on measures of muscle hypertrophy: A systematic review. European Journal of Sport Science, 2017. systematic review · Adults (general)
    Short and long rests can both build muscle; in trained people, longer rests may have an advantage.
  • Hunter, 2014 · Sex differences in human fatigability: mechanisms and insight to physiological responses. Acta Physiologica, 2014. narrative review · Women and men
    Women are usually less fatigable than men in similar-intensity isometric tasks, but the difference depends on the task.

Limits: Most rest-interval trials studied men. Women are often less fatigable in some tasks, so shorter rest may work for some women on some moves. This is an open question.

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Muscle can be built with lighter or heavier weights; the app uses moderate reps (8–15) and progresses load for strength.

  • Schoenfeld et al., 2017 · Strength and Hypertrophy Adaptations Between Low- vs. High-Load Resistance Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 2017. meta-analysis · Adults (general)
    Muscle growth was similar with low and high loads; max strength gains favored high loads.

Limits: Light loads only build similar muscle when sets are taken close to failure. Heavier loads are better for maximal strength.

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Cardio days use steady, easy cardio; sprint drills are optional add-ons, not the main fat-loss tool.

  • Steele et al., 2021 · Slow and Steady, or Hard and Fast? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Studies Comparing Body Composition Changes between Interval Training and Moderate Intensity Continuous Training. Sports, 2021. meta-analysis · Adults (general)
    Interval training and steady moderate cardio produced practically the same fat loss.

Limits: A widely shared 2019 review that favored HIIT for fat loss was retracted; we do not use it.

Sculpting

Limited evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Glute days use hip thrusts and squats/lunges for overall glute size, plus abduction work for the side (upper) glutes.

  • Plotkin et al., 2023 · Hip thrust and back squat training elicit similar gluteus muscle hypertrophy and transfer similarly to the deadlift. Frontiers in Physiology, 2023. randomized trial · Women and men
    Hip thrusts and squats grew the glutes by similar amounts; squats also grew the thighs more.
  • Distefano et al., 2009 · Gluteal muscle activation during common therapeutic exercises. Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2009. lab study · Women and men
    Side-lying hip abduction produced the highest gluteus medius activation of the exercises tested.

Limits: The hip thrust vs squat trial was in untrained young adults over 9 weeks. Muscle-activation (EMG) studies show which moves light up a muscle, which does not always predict growth.

Sculpting builds muscle where you choose, but the app never promises fat loss from one spot.

  • Vispute et al., 2011 · The Effect of Abdominal Exercise on Abdominal Fat. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 2011. randomized trial · Women and men
    6 weeks of ab exercises alone did not reduce belly fat or waist size, though ab endurance improved.

Limits: The best-known trial is small (24 people, 6 weeks). Other studies point the same way, but more research in women would help.

Cycle

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Cycle sync learns from your own check-ins and suggests changes. It never automatically cuts your training by cycle phase.

  • McNulty et al., 2020 · The Effects of Menstrual Cycle Phase on Exercise Performance in Eumenorrheic Women: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Medicine, 2020. meta-analysis · Studied in women
    Performance might be trivially lower in the early follicular phase; the authors recommend a personalized approach based on each woman’s response.
  • Colenso-Semple et al., 2023 · Current evidence shows no influence of women’s menstrual cycle phase on acute strength performance or adaptations to resistance exercise training. Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, 2023. umbrella review · Studied in women
    It is premature to conclude that cycle-phase hormone changes meaningfully affect strength or muscle growth from training.

Limits: The research quality on cycle phase and performance is rated low, and individual women vary a lot. That is exactly why the app personalizes instead of prescribing.

Safety

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Every session has a warm-up and strength work for the whole body. Cool-down stretches are for comfort.

  • Lauersen et al., 2014 · The effectiveness of exercise interventions to prevent sports injuries: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2014. meta-analysis · Women and men
    Strength training reduced sports injuries to less than a third; stretching did not prevent injuries.

Limits: Stretching did not reduce injuries in this analysis, so we do not claim our cool-down prevents injury. Sport drills are low-volume and placed early, before fatigue.

Expert consensusv1 · checked 2026-11-23

Fat-loss targets are gentle (at most ~15% below maintenance) and never go below 1,400 calories a day in the app.

  • Mountjoy et al. (IOC), 2023 · 2023 International Olympic Committee’s (IOC) consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs). British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2023. consensus statement · Women and men
    Eating too little for the amount of exercise you do (low energy availability) harms many body systems, including hormones, bone and mental health.

Limits: The 1,400-calorie floor is our safety choice; it does not come from this paper. The consensus statement explains why under-fueling while training harms health.

Nutrition

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Your protein target is about 1.6 g per kg of body weight per day (capped between 90 and 180 g).

  • Morton et al., 2018 · A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength in healthy adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2018. meta-analysis · Adults (general)
    Across 49 trials, gains in lean mass plateaued at around 1.6 g/kg/day of total protein.

Limits: The cap and floor are our practical limits. Needs vary with age, training and health; ask a dietitian if you have kidney disease or other conditions.

Strong evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

Calories are estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, adjusted for training days and your goal.

  • Frankenfield et al., 2005 · Comparison of Predictive Equations for Resting Metabolic Rate in Healthy Nonobese and Obese Adults: A Systematic Review. Journal of the American Dietetic Association, 2005. systematic review · Adults (general)
    Mifflin-St Jeor was the most reliable equation, predicting resting metabolism within 10% for more people than the others, but errors exist for individuals.

Limits: Any equation can be off by 10% or more for an individual. Treat the number as a starting point and adjust based on how you feel and your progress.

Supplements

Moderate evidencev1 · checked 2026-11-23

The shop lists creatine monohydrate at 3–5 g a day, certified products only, with a "check with your doctor" note.

  • Kreider et al. (ISSN), 2017 · International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation in exercise, sport, and medicine. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2017. position stand · Adults (general)
    Creatine is safe and well tolerated in healthy people in the studied doses, and can improve high-intensity performance and training adaptations.
  • Smith-Ryan et al., 2021 · Creatine Supplementation in Women’s Health: A Lifespan Perspective. Nutrients, 2021. narrative review · Studied in women
    Creatine appears effective for strength and exercise performance in pre-menopausal women; women have lower natural creatine stores.

Limits: Research in women is growing but still smaller than in men. Pregnancy and kidney conditions need a doctor’s input.

How claims are checked

  1. Sources first. We prefer systematic reviews, meta-analyses and position stands, then randomized trials, then lab studies.
  2. Women in the data. We record who each source studied and flag when women weren't included.
  3. Retractions. Every review checks whether a source has been retracted or corrected.
  4. Disputes. Anyone can dispute a claim. We reply, log the decision publicly, and update the claim's version if it changes.
